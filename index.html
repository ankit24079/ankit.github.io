About Me

Research in robotics, perception, and autonomous systems

I am a Master's student in Robotics at the University of Minnesota and a Research Assistant in the Flow Field Imaging Laboratory, where I have been working under the supervision of Professor Jiarong Hong since May 2025. My research lies at the intersection of robotics, computer vision, perception, and autonomous systems, with a primary focus on wildfire ember transport, drone-based sensing, stereo vision, and 3D reconstruction.

My current research involves developing autonomous drone platforms equipped with stereo cameras to capture and analyze airborne ember transport in real-world wildfire environments. I design perception pipelines for ember detection, tracking, triangulation, and 3D trajectory reconstruction, while deploying computer vision models on NVIDIA Jetson platforms for real-time operation. This work combines robotics, environmental sensing, and AI to better understand wildfire behavior and improve future monitoring capabilities.

Beyond my research, I have developed autonomous navigation and perception systems using ROS2, RTAB-Map, Nav2, AMCL, LiDAR localization, Isaac Sim, and Gazebo. My projects include SLAM, autonomous mobile robot navigation, drone autonomy, embedded AI deployment, and VTOL simulation using MAVROS and ArduPilot. I enjoy building robotic systems end-to-end, from hardware integration and sensor calibration to perception, autonomy, and deployment.

Prior to joining the University of Minnesota, I worked as an Assistant Manager at Jindal Steel & Power from June 2022 to June 2024, where I led engineering and industrial automation initiatives in a large-scale manufacturing environment.

My long-term goal is to develop intelligent robotic systems that bridge perception, autonomy, and real-world deployment, with a particular interest in robotics perception, computer vision, SLAM, and autonomous aerial systems.
