Featured Research

Airborne Wildfire Ember Tracking and Autonomous Drone Perception

From stereo ember detection to 3D trajectory reconstruction and drone-following control.

This project develops a drone-based stereo vision system to detect airborne wildfire embers, reconstruct their 3D trajectories, analyze how their velocity changes with height, and use that motion information to guide autonomous drone following for longer-range ember observation.

1. Hardware and Data Collection

Drone platform for airborne ember sensing

A UAV platform was equipped with GPS and a stereo vision camera to capture ember motion from the air. The goal is to move from fixed-camera observation toward mobile drone-based ember tracking.

2. Perception Pipeline

From image stream to motion-informed autonomy

Stereo Image Capture Ember Detection Tracking Stereo Matching Triangulation 3D Trajectory Reconstruction Velocity Analysis Drone Following

The image stream is processed to detect ember streaks, associate detections across frames, match left and right stereo observations, and triangulate 3D ember positions before extracting motion cues for autonomous following.

3. Synthetic Data and Model Training

Improving robustness in low-light wildfire scenes

Synthetic ember images were generated to increase training diversity and improve detection robustness in low-light wildfire scenes. Augmentations included rotation, flipping, brightness variation, color shifts, and synthetic ember insertion.

4. Ember Detection

Detecting ember regions and endpoints

The detection model predicts ember regions and endpoint locations from low-light frames. These detections provide the input for tracking and stereo reconstruction.

5. Tracking and Stereo Matching

Associating motion through time and across cameras

Detected embers are temporally associated across frames to form motion tracks.

Stereo matching then pairs corresponding ember observations between camera views before triangulation.

6. 3D Trajectory Reconstruction

Recovering ember motion in 3D space

Matched stereo observations are triangulated into 3D space, producing ember trajectories that reveal how embers rise, spread, and move through the flow field.

7. Velocity Analysis and Drone Following

Using height-dependent motion to guide autonomous following

After reconstructing ember trajectories, vertical velocity and resultant velocity were evaluated as a function of height. This helps identify how ember motion changes through the transport layer, including regions where particles accelerate or maintain stronger upward motion.

The velocity profile is important because it gives a reference for how fast the drone should move vertically to keep embers in view. Initial autonomous tests used onboard perception and offboard control to command the drone to follow a detected target, validating the foundation for future ember-following experiments.

  • Velocity analysis informs vertical drone motion
  • Perception output provides target location and motion cues
  • Drone follows the target in offboard or autonomous mode
  • Goal is to capture longer ember trajectories from the air
